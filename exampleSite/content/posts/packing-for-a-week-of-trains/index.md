Everything I take on a week of trains fits in a 30-litre rucksack. That is not a rule I set out to follow; it is what fits under a train seat and in the luggage net of a couchette, and I got tired of carrying more.

## The bag

A 30-litre rucksack with a clamshell opening, so I can reach the bottom without unpacking on a platform. Mine weighs 900 g empty. Anything bigger ends up in the rack at the end of the carriage, out of sight.

## Clothes for seven days

- Three T-shirts in merino, which can be worn twice and dry overnight
- One long-sleeved shirt for dinners
- One merino jumper, the warmest thing I take
- Two pairs of trousers, one of them light enough to sleep in
- Four pairs of socks and underwear, washed in the sink
- One light rain jacket that packs into its own pocket

## For the night trains

A silk sleeping-bag liner (couchette sheets are thin), earplugs, an eye mask, and a small bag with everything I need in the morning, so I never have to open the big one in a compartment of six.

## The small things

- A two-port charger and one cable that does everything
- A power bank, because sockets are where you are not
- A spork and a collapsible cup
- A paper copy of every ticket and reservation

## The three things I always forget

A plug adapter for Switzerland, which uses its own sockets. A pen, for the border forms that still exist. And a bag for the food I buy at the station before a long day, which always ends up loose in the rucksack.
