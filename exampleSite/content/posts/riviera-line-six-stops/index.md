The coast line from Nice to La Spezia is 240 km of tunnels with the sea in between. Ride it straight through and it takes five and a half hours with two changes. Ride it the way I did in late October, over four days with a small bag, and it is the best slow week I have had on a train this year.

## Before you go

You need three tickets, not one. French TER trains run from Nice to Ventimiglia, then Trenitalia regional trains take over. Neither sells the other's tickets online, so buy the French leg on the SNCF Connect app and the Italian legs at the machine in Ventimiglia. Regional tickets in Italy are not tied to a train: validate them in the green machine on the platform, or the conductor will fine you €50 even with a ticket in your hand.

Sit on the **left** heading east. That is the sea side for nearly the whole run.

## Nice to Menton

Half an hour along the Côte d'Azur, the sea so close the spray reaches the tracks at Cap d'Ail. Menton is the place to stop for a night: the old town climbs straight up from the station and the lemon trees are not a slogan.

## Ventimiglia

Change here and leave the station quickly. Friday is market day, and the covered market behind the station sells the best focaccia I ate all week, for €2.50 a slab.

## Genova

The halfway point, and the only city on the line. Genova Piazza Principe has a left-luggage desk on the lower level (€6 for five hours), which is what makes a day in the alleys of the old port possible with a bag.

## Camogli and Santa Margherita

Two regional stops ten minutes apart. Camogli has the painted houses; Santa Margherita has the bus to Portofino and the cheaper rooms. I stayed in Santa Margherita and walked over the headland to Camogli: three hours, steep, worth it.

## The Cinque Terre

From Levanto to La Spezia the Cinque Terre Express runs every 15 to 20 minutes. The day card (€19.50 off season) covers the trains and the coastal paths. Go early. By eleven the platforms at Monterosso are full, and the trains run late.
